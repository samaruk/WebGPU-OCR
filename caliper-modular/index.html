02a Borders · rules

Runs the rule detector of section 08 (same k for border binary, gap-bridge, opening, thickness, coverage and dashed settings) on the rectified image. Rules are interpreted as a full table grid, a vertical grid, a boxed header row, stacked row rules or section separators, and their pixels are erased before glyph detection so a rule fused to a glyph never spoils a line.
A horizontal rule at least this fraction of the page width (or a vertical one of the page height) counts as long: only long rules can form stacked row rules; the grid uses every rule that intersects another.
A long rule outside the table at least this wide is a section separator.
Erasing paints every detected rule — table borders, section lines, pen lines, dashed rules — out of the working image with the surrounding paper. Text lines, columns, pass A, deskew, dewarp and passes B and C then process the rule-free image; pass B\u2019s own border detection still reads the original. With a full or vertical grid the column stage takes the table band from the border box; with a header box or a grid it adds the vertical rules as column boundaries where the glyph coverage does not contradict them.

02b Text lines · clean

Runs on the rectified image. Character components are filtered, chained into text lines, and only the ink of accepted lines is passed on: pass A runs on this clean binary instead of thresholding the image itself. Chaining (not dilation) is used so that skewed lines are followed and stacked lines never fuse; a pen tick or stroke that reaches two lines is detected as a bridge and can never join them.
Components shorter than this fraction of the median component height are noise: dust, halftone dots, thin rules.
Taller components are cut at their ink valleys; whatever is still taller (logos, vertical rules, box borders, uncuttable merges) is removed.
Width ÷ height above this is rule-shaped. Touching characters in bold or small fonts rarely exceed 6–8.
Two components on the same line are linked when the horizontal gap between them is at most this multiple of the taller one. Word spaces are ~0.3–0.6; column gutters are usually 2+.
Linked components must overlap vertically by this fraction of the shorter one. Lower follows steeper skew; higher separates tightly stacked lines more strictly.
Chains with fewer components are rejected. At 1, a lone component is still accepted only if it looks like a glyph (height, fill and aspect checks).

02c Columns

Works on the full lines of 02b in the de-skewed frame. Rows with enough pieces form the table band; a coverage profile of the glyphs across that band exposes the gutters; the intervals between gutters are the columns and every glyph is assigned to the column under its centre. Needs no rules or borders.
A full line with at least this many pieces counts as a table row. Key/value rows like "Invoice Date : 16.05.2026" have 2 pieces, so 3 keeps them out of the band.
How many consecutive non-tabular rows (blank or wrapped cells) may sit inside the band without ending it.
An invoice has one item table. When a watermark, a pen line or a paper fold damages a few rows, the table shows up as two separate runs of tabular rows; a second run within this many rows of the main one is merged back in if its glyphs respect the main run's gutters (same columns), together with the damaged rows in between. 0 disables merging.
A valley narrower than this cannot be a gutter. A gutter is either a clear run (coverage below the max below) or a deep valley — coverage at most 42 % of the lower neighbouring peak — which is how columns separated only by a word-space-sized gap that lines up in every row are found. Word spaces inside a column fall at different positions per row and never dip that deep.
A gutter may be crossed by at most this fraction of the band rows (a long product name spilling into the next column). 0 demands a perfectly clear gutter.

05 Height filter · pass A

A blob taller than this multiple of the median blob height spans more than one line: its ink is projected onto Y and cut at every genuine valley, and each piece becomes its own blob. Any blob or piece still taller than this after the cut is a merge that could not be separated and is removed. Blobs wider than the section 06 max aspect ratio (× their height) are also dropped as rule-shaped. Runs on the before-rotate pass only, right after the min-area filter, so contours, hulls, calipers and OBBs are computed on the survivors.
Blobs shorter than this fraction of the median blob height are removed — dots, dashes, speckle and thin horizontal rules.

05b Line blobs · pass A

Horizontal growth applied to the blobs that survived section 05, so that neighbouring words on the same line merge into one component. Set it larger than the widest word gap on the page and smaller than the column gutter you want to keep separate.
Vertical growth. Keep it far below the line gap so stacked lines stay separate. Because rules, borders, logos and multi-line merges were already removed in section 05, the fused components are clean whole text lines; the stage shows each line with its word count.

05c Full lines · pass A

Two line blobs from section 05b are joined into one full line when their ink overlaps vertically by at least this fraction of the shorter one. Each piece is judged against its nearest piece in the row, so a gently curled row still joins end to end. A join is always refused when the two neighbours together would exceed the section 05 max line height, and also whenever the two pieces overlap horizontally: reading left to right there is exactly one piece at any horizontal position, so pieces that share an x range are different lines no matter how little they are offset. The result is one line per text row spanning from its leftmost to its rightmost piece.

06 Non-character filter

Hollow box borders fill little of their OBB.

07 Split merged boxes

A box taller than this multiple of the median word height is cut at the line gap; if a piece is still too tall it is re-dilated with reduced Dilation H (twice), then flagged rejected. After-rotate pass; accepted and rejected boxes alike.

07b Height-density filter

All accepted blob heights are bucketed; the peak bucket defines the dominant text-line height. The band is grown outward while density stays above peak × this fraction. Blobs shorter than the band are rejected as noise; blobs taller are split at internal valleys and rejected when no in-range split is possible. Lower threshold → wider band (more permissive); higher → narrower band (more aggressive cleanup).

08 Table layout

Higher splits the table into more, narrower columns; lower needs a wider whitespace gutter. Word boxes are grouped into rows, and the dominant multi-column band becomes the table — blank or wrapped-text rows inside it are kept — while the header / footer fall outside.
Sauvola threshold weight used for the SEPARATE border-detection binary. Lower than k means more pixels are classified as ink, which helps thin / faint rules survive — they are often dropped at the higher k value tuned for word blobs. The word-detection binary stage (06) keeps using k. Set equal to k to skip the second Sauvola pass.
Border detection · solid rules
Rules are extracted by bridging gaps in each row/column, then morphological opening keeps only locally-near-horizontal (or near-vertical) structures, then per-component centerline tracing gives a polyline.
A rule must span ≥ this fraction of image width (h-rules) or height (v-rules). Smaller catches shorter rules; larger filters out short text-like horizontals.
Per-row / per-column gap filling. Gaps up to this many pixels between consecutive ink runs in the same row/column are bridged. Set ≥ the widest text glyph that typically crosses a rule (usually 8-15 px). Doesn't extend past the rule's natural endpoints.
Erosion+dilation radius. Larger = more aggressive text rejection but less curvature tolerance. Max tolerable slope at any local point ≈ lineThickness / K. Lower this if real borders have sharper curves; raise it if text strokes are leaking through as false rules.
Discards rules whose mean per-column / per-row ink height exceeds this. Genuine rules are 1-3 px thick; long horizontal text "lines" register as 6-15. Raise for thick decorative rules.
Fraction of the rule's polyline points whose location coincides with ink in the original binary. Real rules score ~1.0; a row of disjointed shards or sparse dots that the gap-bridging artificially joined scores lower. Raise to 0.85+ to reject everything but the cleanest rules.
Sliding-window radius for smoothing the polyline along its axis. Removes the residual pixel-level jitter that's left after body-column detection has already excluded text-overlap regions. 0 disables.
Border detection · dashed / dotted rules
Small connected components (dots) clustered into chains with uniform size and stride. Chains failing periodicity, size-consistency, or stride-to-size gates are rejected. Drawn in the gallery as a thin transparent line with explicit dot markers.
A CCA component qualifies as a dot only if its bbox fits inside this in BOTH dimensions. Most text is 8-20 px; lowering this to 3-4 keeps only true dots, while 6 also catches short dashes.
Minimum number of components in a chain. Higher → fewer false positives from coincidentally-aligned isolated marks.
The key discriminator between a dashed rule and a tightly-packed row of small text characters. A real dashed rule has stride ≫ dot size; text has stride ~ character width. Raise to 2.5+ if text-row false positives appear.
Total span (last dot − first dot) must reach this fraction of the image side. Stricter than the solid-rule cutoff because dashed patterns at short lengths are usually coincidental, not real borders.
Pass B · row dilation — H large fuses words into a full text-line; V small heals descenders/ascenders without bridging stacked lines.
Pass C · column dilation — V large fuses a column's stacked rows into one stripe; H small glues a row's word-fragments together without bridging side-by-side columns.
Rows come from Pass B blobs and columns come from Pass C blobs. The legacy AABB-level union-find (RLSA on word boxes) and whitespace-projection paths have been replaced by pixel-level CCA on the directionally-dilated binaries.
